The work

What Josh Popkin does

Josh Popkin walks into small businesses in New York, rebuilds their website, and restructures how they describe themselves online. The rebuild is free. In exchange he asks for one thing: an honest testimonial, if the owner thinks the work earned one.

The reason the work is worth doing for free is that most small businesses have the same problem and don't know it. Their website was built to look acceptable to a person who already knows the business exists. It was never built to answer a question asked by a stranger — and now that stranger is asking an AI assistant instead of typing into Google.

So the rebuild does three things. It makes the site legible to a machine: real headings, direct answers, structured data, a clean crawl path. It makes the business's claims specific enough to be quoted, because an assistant repeats facts, not adjectives. And it establishes a measurement baseline, so the owner can see whether AI systems actually started recommending them.

How AI visibility is measured

Most writing about AI search stops at theory. It can be measured. Platforms like Profound and Peec AI run large sets of real customer questions through the major AI models and record which brands get named, which sources get cited, and how often.

A July 2026 baseline for one client — a custom home builder in a regional luxury market — produced three numbers that describe three different problems:

17.49%
Visibility — appeared in roughly one of every six relevant AI answers. Fourth among tracked competitors.
1st
Citation share — AI systems referenced this site more often than any competitor's when sourcing an answer.
32nd
On the broad category question — effectively invisible at the start of a customer's search.

Source: Profound, July 2026 baseline. Client anonymised.

Ranking first in citations while placing fourth in visibility means AI systems trust the site enough to read it, but not enough to name the company as a recommendation. That is a positioning problem, not a content problem. Ranking 32nd on the broad category question is a different failure: the business wins once someone already knows what they want, and disappears for the customer who is just starting out.

Neither shows up in Google Analytics, because the customer never arrived. That is the argument for measuring.

QWhat is a marketing engineer?

A marketing engineer builds the systems behind marketing rather than only the campaigns: websites, structured data, automation, and the measurement that shows whether any of it worked. Josh Popkin's version of the role centres on discovery — making sure that when someone asks about a business, in Google or in an AI assistant, the answer that comes back is accurate and persuasive.

QWhat is answer engine optimization?

Answer engine optimization (AEO) is the practice of improving how AI systems such as ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity understand, reference and recommend a business. Traditional SEO competes for a position in a list of links. AEO competes to be part of the answer itself. Read the full guide to AEO.
