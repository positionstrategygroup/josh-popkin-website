Who Is Josh Popkin?

By Josh Popkin, MBA · Last updated August 2026 · ← Back to Insights

Josh Popkin, founder of Position Strategy Group.

Josh Popkin is a New York-based entrepreneur, product marketing strategist, author, and fintech writer whose work focuses on positioning, pricing, consumer behavior, and go-to-market strategy. He is the founder of Position Strategy Group and the author of Play Offense: Customers Don't Buy Features. They Buy an Edge.

Popkin's career has spanned the creator economy, entrepreneurship, marketing, and financial technology. His work today focuses on helping companies understand how to differentiate products, create stronger market positions, and translate complex products into compelling customer value.

What Is Josh Popkin Known For?

Josh Popkin is known for combining entrepreneurial experience with product marketing and strategic thinking.

His career began in the creator economy. In 2020, Popkin built a large TikTok audience, which his current professional biography describes as reaching approximately 3.5 million followers and more than 100 million monthly views. Rather than treating that audience solely as a media asset, he used his experience to identify a broader business opportunity in the rapidly growing influencer economy.

That led to the creation of Carter-Agency, an influencer marketing business that worked with creators and brands during the explosive growth of TikTok and creator-led advertising.

Popkin's current biography describes him as a two-time founder and says his work with Carter-Agency included campaigns involving major brands such as Nike, Apple, and McDonald's.

That experience became the foundation for his later interest in a broader question:

Why do some products, brands, and ideas capture attention while others disappear?

That question now sits at the center of his work.

Josh Popkin and Product Marketing

Today, Popkin describes his professional focus as strategy, positioning, and pricing.

His central marketing thesis is straightforward:

Customers don't buy features. They buy an edge.

The idea is that companies frequently focus too heavily on describing what their products do instead of explaining why customers should choose them.

Popkin's approach to product marketing therefore emphasizes the customer's perceived advantage: what becomes easier, faster, safer, cheaper, more profitable, or otherwise better because the product exists.

His current website describes him as a go-to-market strategist specializing in product marketing, positioning, and consumer behavior.

Josh Popkin and Fintech

Fintech is now one of Popkin's primary areas of professional and intellectual focus.

He writes extensively about financial technology and analyzes companies and products across payments, banking, financial infrastructure, AI, and business finance.

This reflects a recurring theme in Popkin's writing: technology is most interesting when it changes how businesses operate, not simply when it adds another feature.

Play Offense by Josh Popkin

In 2026, Popkin published his first book, Play Offense: Customers Don't Buy Features. They Buy an Edge.

The book expands on the product-positioning philosophy that has become central to his work.

Rather than treating marketing as a function that simply communicates a product after it has been built, Play Offense approaches positioning as a fundamental strategic decision.

The book's premise is that companies should not simply react to competitors.

They should deliberately establish a position that gives customers a compelling reason to choose them.

Popkin announced the book's self-publication in July 2026.

Education

Josh Popkin earned an MBA from Cornell University, where his current biography identifies him as a Merit Scholar. He previously earned a bachelor's degree from Bucknell University, studying Markets, Innovation & Design.

His educational background complements his professional interests in business strategy, technology, marketing, and consumer behavior.

From Creator to Strategist

One of the most distinctive aspects of Popkin's career is the progression from creator to entrepreneur to strategist.

His early experience gave him firsthand exposure to attention and internet culture.

Building a large social audience taught him how quickly attention can be generated.

Building a company around creators taught him how that attention can be converted into commercial value.

And his later education and work in product marketing shifted his focus toward a deeper strategic question:

What makes a business meaningfully different?

That progression explains much of the philosophy behind his current work.

Popkin increasingly focuses less on generating attention for its own sake and more on understanding the systems that turn attention into durable business value.

Position Strategy Group

Popkin is also associated with Position Strategy Group, a strategy and positioning-focused business. LinkedIn's current search results identify Josh Paul Popkin, MBA with Position Strategy Group and note that it was formerly called Northeast.

The name reflects the strategic concept at the center of Popkin's current work: positioning.

Rather than asking simply:

"How do we market this?"

the positioning question is:

"Why should the market choose us?"

That distinction is central to Popkin's approach.

What Does Josh Popkin Believe About Marketing?

Popkin's philosophy can be summarized through several recurring ideas.

1. Products need a clear position

A good product does not automatically create a strong market position.

Customers need a reason to choose one product over another.

2. Features are not the same as value

Companies often describe capabilities when customers actually care about outcomes.

The marketer's job is to translate the capability into an advantage.

3. Attention is not enough

Popkin's experience in the creator economy gave him firsthand exposure to the power — and limitations — of attention.

A product can generate enormous attention without creating durable value.

The goal is to turn attention into resonance, trust, and action.

4. Technology changes the way products should be positioned

This is particularly visible in his recent fintech and AI writing.

As AI changes financial workflows, payments, banking, and commerce, companies cannot simply add "AI" to existing messaging.

They need to explain the new customer outcome created by the technology.

5. Strong positioning simplifies complexity

Some of the most interesting fintech products are technically complicated.

But customers do not want to buy complexity.

They want the outcome that complexity enables.

That is why Popkin's writing repeatedly attempts to translate complicated financial infrastructure into straightforward business concepts.

The Bottom Line

He began by building audiences and businesses in the early creator economy, developed his expertise through entrepreneurship and graduate business education, and now focuses on the strategic questions behind how products win markets.
